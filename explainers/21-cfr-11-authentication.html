Compliant

splitscreen§ 11.200 Analysis (Components)

This model is compliant because the ID and Password are "distinct components." The password is a secret known only to the user and is stored separately from their email account.

Regulation §11.200(a)(1): "Electronic signatures that are not based upon biometrics shall... Employ at least two distinct identification components such as an identification code and password."

security§ 11.300 Analysis (Risks & Controls)

Primary Risk: Static Credential Theft

The password is static and can be stolen via phishing. The "Forgot Password" flow via email is a key vulnerability.

Mitigation:

Risk is managed through rigorous procedural controls: documented SOPs for loss management, strict identity verification for password resets, and security notifications.

Regulation §11.300(d): "...Use of transaction safeguards to prevent unauthorized use of passwords and/or identification codes, and to detect and report in an immediate and urgent manner any attempts at their unauthorized use..."

Compliant (Highly Secure)

This is a very strong and widely accepted model.

splitscreen§ 11.200 Analysis (Components)

This is a clear example of two distinct components. They rely on separate communication channels (email vs. SMS) and separate devices (computer vs. phone). Compromising one does not compromise the other.

Regulation §11.200(a)(1): "Electronic signatures that are not based upon biometrics shall... Employ at least two distinct identification components such as an identification code and password."

security§ 11.300 Analysis (Risks & Controls)

Primary Risk: Credential Change / SIM Swap

An attacker could use social engineering to trick a mobile carrier into transferring the phone number to a new SIM card.

Mitigation:

This is an external risk. Internally, the system must have rigorous procedural controls for changing a registered phone number, requiring stringent identity proofing before any update.

Regulation §11.300(c): "Following loss management procedures to electronically deauthorize lost, stolen, missing, or otherwise potentially compromised tokens...and to issue temporary or permanent replacements using suitable, rigorous controls."

Non-Compliant

splitscreen§ 11.200 Analysis (Components)

This method is non-compliant because it is a single-factor system. The "access code" is just one component. It lacks a second *distinct* component that is uniquely tied to the individual signer.

Regulation §11.100(a): "Each electronic signature shall be unique to one individual and shall not be reused by, or reassigned to, anyone else."

security§ 11.300 Analysis (Risks & Controls)

Primary Risk: Shared Secret & Transmission Risk

The code is a shared secret, not unique to the user. Its security depends entirely on how the sender transmits it. It can be shared or forwarded, breaking non-repudiation.

Mitigation:

This model's security rests on human action, which is not a valid technical control. The risk cannot be systematically mitigated to meet compliance standards.

This model is non-compliant because the two components are not distinct. Both the identifier and the authenticator are controlled by a single asset: the email inbox. This collapses two security layers into one.

Regulation §11.200(a)(1): "Electronic signatures that are not based upon biometrics shall... Employ at least two distinct identification components such as an identification code and password."

security§ 11.300 Analysis (Risks & Controls)

Primary Risk: Single Channel Compromise

If an attacker gains access to the user's email, they have everything needed to forge a signature.

Mitigation:

This fundamental flaw cannot be mitigated by procedural controls because it fails the technical requirement of §11.200 at the moment of signing.
